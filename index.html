Built prototype

Making attention visible.

The existing mechanical demo uses an ESP32, three SG90 servos, and 3D-printed parts to fold, rise, and retract. Its structure was adapted from an earlier project and developed through model iterations.

The current ultrasonic sensor and buzzer are prototype components. They do not provide the proposed camera and microphone functions.

Proposed extension

From motion to dialogue.

A camera would send video to a computer running MediaPipe. An instrumented Android shopping task would supply actual touch timestamps. A XiaoZhi-based voice service with Qwen would support dialogue, with MCP tool calls requesting bounded motion from the ESP32.

Vision, AI integration, and user evaluation remain planned work. Hand motion is a contextual cue; it neither proves screen contact nor directly measures indecision.

Research references & interpretation
  1. OpenStax, Calculus Volume 2, §3.7. Gabriel’s Horn is used as a mathematical metaphor for finite capacity and an endless surface, not as a model of human cognition.
  2. Ferguson (1989), Who Solved the Secretary Problem? The 1/e stopping threshold assumes a known number of randomly ordered options and irreversible choices. It is not a shopping rule.
  3. Irie et al. (2022), Mental arithmetic modulates temporal variabilities of finger-tapping tasks in a tempo-dependent manner. Supports task-dependent differences in timing variability, not a direct detector of hesitation.
  4. Holm et al. (2017), Executive control and working memory are involved in sub-second repetitive motor timing. Laboratory memory-load findings motivate a design hypothesis that still needs validation in phone use.
